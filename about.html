The Name

“Nawiri” means to bloom. It is a word that carries patience, reflecting the understanding that growth does not happen all at once, but in seasons and with the right conditions. It is rooted, gradual, and real. We exist to create those conditions because mental wellness is not a destination or a cure. It is a living, ongoing process of becoming, both for individuals ready to begin and for communities that have been waiting too long.

Our Model

How We Work: Profit for Purpose

Nawiri operates as a hybrid social enterprise. Nawiri Akili-Care Africa, our registered consultancy, generates professional revenue through therapy and organisational wellness services. That revenue directly supports the Nawiri Akili Initiative, which serves as our community impact arm.

Community sponsorships and corporate partnerships are received and managed through the consultancy while the Initiative's formal CBO registration is being finalised. Every contribution is tracked independently to ensure community-first sponsorships go directly toward subsidised care, school programs, and free community sessions.

We believe professional expertise should fuel social change. Thank you for partnering with us to help communities bloom.

Founder

Built from experience. Driven by purpose.

Nawiri was born from a professional understanding, a caring heart, and a mind that has firsthand known the weight of struggling without support.

Peris Gichuki is a licensed psychologist with over five years of clinical and community experience spanning psychotherapy, psychosocial counselling, trauma-informed care, addiction rehabilitation, GBV-integrated support, clinical supervision, and community outreach.

She grew up wishing she had the language to name what she felt, the guidance to make sense of it, and a safe space to speak it without fear.

That wish became a vision, and that vision became Nawiri.

Built for the generation coming up, built for communities that have been waiting too long, and built on the belief that professional care and open, barrier-free access are not two separate goals. They are two halves of the same commitment.
